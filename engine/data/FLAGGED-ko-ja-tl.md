# Titles for Japanese, Korean and Tagalog: what was checked online

Checked on 2026-10-14 against the pages below. An entry is `checked` when its formal term was found on a page; `unverified` means no page was found for it. Spoken forms are noted where they were not checked. None of this replaces a native speaker (decision 14); every entry still needs one.

Sources:

- Korean: [Indiana University, Korean kinship terms](https://korean.indiana.edu/K102/L14kinterms.html); [thewiki.kr, kinship chart (7촌)](https://thewiki.kr/w/7촌); [촌수와 친척 간 호칭](https://m.cafe.daum.net/maranada91/Osob/42); [hangyo.com, 호칭·지칭어](https://hangyo.com/mobile/article.html?no=697); [TOPIK GUIDE, family-in-law terms](https://www.topikguide.com/?p=47612); [transparent.com, Korean family terms](https://blogs.transparent.com/korean/?p=6583).
- Japanese: [Omniglot, Japanese kinship terms](https://omniglot.com/language/kinship/japanese.htm); [romajidesu, 大伯父](https://romajidesu.com/dictionary/meaning-of-大伯父.html); [weblio, 再従弟](https://www.weblio.jp/content/再従弟).
- Tagalog: [Wikipedia, Philippine kinship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_kinship); [tagalognow.com, family words](https://www.tagalognow.com/learn/filipino-family-words-and-honorifics/); [tagalog.com, lolo sa tuhod](https://tagalog.com/dictionary/lolo-sa-tuhod).

What the check changed: Tagalog second cousins are `pinsang makalawa` (not "pangalawang pinsan") and first cousins `pinsang buo`; `pamangkíng` and `apong` take the linker the sources show. Japanese parents' cousins are いとこおじ / いとこおば, not 従伯叔父. Korean entries were confirmed as listed, with notes on reference versus address.

What is still unchecked: Korean 친사촌/외사촌 and cousin compounds, 친할아버지, 시/처 grandparents, 외손자 and 친손자, 며느리 and 사위 (standard words, not on the pages read); Japanese 従兄/従弟, 嫁, 婿, 曾祖父, 義理の祖父, and which of 大伯父 and 大叔父 is the older brother; every Tagalog term for a grandparent's sibling (no source says), and tita or tito for an aunt's or uncle's spouse.
